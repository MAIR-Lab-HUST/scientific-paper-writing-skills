# Evidence-Led Paper Writing

Use this skill to turn research material into a defensible paper argument, or to find and repair breaks in one. Work from supplied or independently verified facts; when support is missing, keep the gap visible rather than filling it with a fluent guess.

## Core rules

- Label statements by epistemic status: **observation** (directly reported fact), **hypothesis** (testable proposal), or **supported explanation** (interpretation backed by stated evidence).
- Keep the argument chain explicit: problem → testable claim → method → evidence → scope. Do not strengthen any link beyond what the record supports.
- Preserve reported numbers, units, conditions, citations, and uncertainty. State transformations, exclusions, or comparability limits instead of silently smoothing them away.
- Test plausible alternative explanations and identify the smallest discriminator test that would separate them.
- Do not imitate a named author's voice. Do not call work current, leading, or state of the art without verified, protocol-matched support.
- Return the smallest useful artifact: a claim map, outline, paragraph, experiment note, or audit finding as the request requires.

## Working loop

1. Establish the requested deliverable and the available record. Separate verified inputs from proposed reasoning and missing evidence.
2. Express the central claim in falsifiable, scoped language, then map each part to a method and a result that can support it.
3. Probe alternatives, protocol mismatches, and uncertainty before presenting an explanatory story.
4. Draft or revise so results report observations first; interpretation, limitations, and scope follow visibly.
5. Perform a final traceability pass: every consequential claim, number, comparison, and citation should have a clear role and support level.

If the record cannot justify the requested conclusion, provide a conditional draft or a focused evidence request instead of inventing support.

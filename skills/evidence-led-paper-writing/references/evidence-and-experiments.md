# Evidence and Experiments

Use this reference when choosing experiments, assessing whether results support a claim, or presenting comparisons without overstating them.

## Turn a claim into a test

For every consequential claim, state:

- the predicted pattern and the conditions under which it should appear;
- the primary outcome, metric, or observation that will be examined;
- the comparison, control, or baseline needed for interpretation;
- the decision rule or failure pattern that would weaken the claim; and
- the intended scope of inference.

Select methods because they distinguish the claim from alternatives, not merely because they generate an appealing result.

## Map evidence with traceability

Record the location, status, and limitations of each evidence item in the [claim-evidence ledger](../assets/claim-evidence-ledger.md). Distinguish raw observation, derived analysis, and interpretation. For a derived value, preserve the source values, transformation, units, and assumptions needed to reproduce its meaning.

Keep uncertainty attached to the result it qualifies: variation, sample size, confidence or interval convention, missingness, threshold choices, protocol deviations, and conditions under which the measurement may not transfer.

## Design discriminator tests

Favor the smallest test that separates plausible accounts. Depending on the claim, this may be a matched control, an ablation, an intervention, a negative case, a perturbation, a held-out condition, or an independent measurement. Specify what each account predicts before seeing the outcome.

If an alternative cannot be tested, do not hide it. Narrow the explanation and identify the unresolved ambiguity.

## Guard comparisons

Comparative statements require a compatible task, data split, metric definition, implementation or measurement protocol, and evaluation conditions. Preserve material differences rather than treating unlike results as a ranking.

Do not claim that a method is current, best, leading, or state of the art unless a verified source and a protocol-matched comparison establish that exact statement. A strong but narrower description is preferable to an unsupported superlative.

## Before reporting results

- Verify that values, units, rounding, and directions match the underlying record.
- Mark exploratory findings as exploratory; do not rewrite them as pre-specified tests.
- Separate a null or inconclusive result from evidence that a mechanism is absent.
- State the evidence's reach before discussing broader implications.

# Section Playbook

Use this reference for section-level drafting or diagnosis. Adapt the structure to the venue and evidence; do not force every paper into the same outline.

## Title and abstract

Name the problem, setting, and contribution without promising more than the evidence shows. In the abstract, connect motivation, approach, principal observation, and scope. Retain material conditions and uncertainty when omission would change the claim.

## Introduction

Define the practical or scientific problem, identify the precise unresolved point, and state the paper's testable contribution. Use citations to support background facts and prior findings, not as decoration. Contributions should be scoped claims, not a feature list.

## Background or related work

Organize prior work around the distinction the paper needs to make. Describe prior methods and findings accurately, preserve protocol differences, and avoid ranking claims unless the comparison has direct support.

## Methods

Make the path from the claim to the test inspectable: materials or data, conditions, controls, procedure, analysis choices, and exclusions. State decisions that affect interpretation. A method description should enable scrutiny, not merely advertise novelty.

## Results

Lead with observations: what was measured, under what conditions, and what the result was. Keep numerical values, units, uncertainty, and comparison basis intact. Place causal or mechanistic interpretation after the reader can see the result it rests on.

## Discussion

Explain how the evidence bears on the claim, then compare it with alternatives. Separate what the study establishes from what it suggests. Name limitations, untested boundary conditions, and the most useful next discriminator test.

## Conclusion and supporting material

Restate only the supported contribution and its scope. Use appendices, supplements, or tables for details needed to audit a claim, reproduce an analysis, or understand a comparison; do not use them to conceal qualification that changes the headline conclusion.

## Drafting modes

- **Outline:** give each heading an evidence role and list missing support.
- **Paragraph:** begin with the claim or observation, provide its support, then state the interpretation and boundary.
- **Diagnosis:** identify the first broken link among problem, claim, method, evidence, scope, and wording.
